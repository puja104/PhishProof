What is Phishing?

Phishing is a type of cyberattack in which attackers try to steal sensitive data by tricking users into revealing personal information. There are multiple types of phishing, including email phishing, smishing, vishing, and whaling. Email phishing involves fake emails that appear to come from a trusted source. Smishing is similar to email phishing, but it is carried out through text messages. The goal of these attacks is to trick individuals into clicking malicious links, downloading harmful attachments, or sharing sensitive information such as passwords, credit card numbers, or personal data. Anyone can be a target of phishing, which is why it is important to learn how to recognize and avoid these attacks.

How to Spot a Phishing Email

1
Check the Sender's Email Address

Look carefully at the full email address, not just the display name. Attackers use addresses that look similar to real ones but with small changes like swapped letters, added numbers, or different domains (e.g., support@micros0ft-alerts.com instead of support@microsoft.com).

2
Look for Urgency and Threats

Phishing emails often pressure you to act fast with phrases like "Your account will be locked," "Respond within 24 hours," or "Immediate action required." Legitimate companies rarely threaten you via email.

3
Hover Over Links Before Clicking

Before clicking any link, hover your mouse over it to see the actual URL. If it doesn't match the company's official website or looks suspicious (random characters, misspellings, unfamiliar domain), don't click it.

4
Watch for Requests for Sensitive Information

No legitimate company will ask you to send passwords, credit card numbers, Social Security numbers, or banking details via email. If an email asks for this, it's a red flag.

5
Be Suspicious of Attachments

Unexpected attachments can contain malware. Be especially cautious of files ending in .exe, .zip, or double extensions like .pdf.exe. If you weren't expecting a file, verify with the sender before opening it.

6
Check for Generic Greetings

Phishing emails often use vague greetings like "Dear User," "Dear Customer," or "Dear Employee" instead of your actual name. Legitimate companies usually address you by name.

7
Look for Spelling and Grammar Errors

While modern phishing emails are getting better, many still contain awkward phrasing, spelling mistakes, or grammar errors that a professional company would not make.

What To Do If You Suspect a Phishing Email

DO:
  • Report it to your IT department
  • Delete the email
  • Verify by contacting the sender directly using a known phone number or website
  • Check the sender's full email address
  • Hover over links to check the real URL
DON'T:
  • Click on any links in the email
  • Download or open attachments
  • Reply to the email
  • Enter your password or personal info
  • Forward it to others (unless reporting)
